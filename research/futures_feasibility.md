# Native index-futures data feasibility

Read-only source inspection on 2026-10-04. No futures strategy was backtested, no trading account was opened and the reserved EURUSD April 2022–September 2024 final sample was not evaluated.

None of the inspected public CME sources currently supplies a verified, freely accessible, multi-year M5/M15 dataset with sufficient contract/roll provenance for the proposed independent validation. This is a result of the inspected sources, not a claim that no such dataset exists. A public Chinese index-futures mirror is a possible alternative, but its original data provider is unidentified.

## A public non-CME alternative worth validating separately

[Freddy-Hexas/china-futures-5min-2015-2025](https://github.com/Freddy-Hexas/china-futures-5min-2015-2025/tree/1a402721d639432875504a343e2b9ffe9508cd36) contains 9,345 CSVs. Its README claims April 16, 2015–June 30, 2025 native five-minute futures data and CC0 licensing. The pinned tree contains **186 IF**, **126 IC** and **126 IH** files with explicit expiry codes, alongside other exchange products. This is structurally a better contract identity record than a single continuous ticker.

The inspected `IC1505.csv` has OHLC, volume, turnover and open interest. Its first timestamp is 2015-04-16 09:15; the last is 2015-05-15 15:10. The final two rows have zero volume and flat prices, so these records are not evidence of executable trades at those times. The README supplies neither original provider nor timestamp convention, bar-start/bar-end semantics, padding policy or independent price cross-check. The uploader's CC0 statement is present, but upstream rights are not independently established. A source-quality audit must precede strategy use. It also needs native CFFEX specifications, CNY accounting, dated brokerage/exchange fees, historical session changes and a declared contract-selection rule; the current USD cash-FX engine does not supply those.

Native MES/MNQ began in May 2019; earlier ES/NQ bars can support a separately disclosed proxy study, not historical claims that a micro contract was traded before launch. Native contracts require the correct expiry and cash-settlement handling. The [CME equity-index price-limit reference](https://www.cmegroup.com/trading/equity-index/us-index/equity-index-price-limits.html) is needed for sessions and order behavior; it is not a promise that a stop order always executes.

No newly verified brokerage fee schedule was obtained. A futures study must freeze a dated, concrete broker schedule, including brokerage, exchange/clearing and regulatory charges per contract **per side**, then add observed or conservative bid/ask spread and slippage in ticks. For example, [Interactive Brokers futures commissions](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/pricing/commissions-futures.php) is a primary reference to verify, not a fee already measured here. FX pip costs and the FX minimum-commission assumption must not silently substitute for futures transaction costs. Increasing virtual equity cannot remove the actual costs of one contract.

## Collateral and the 1% rule

The cash-FX engine's whole-purchase cap is 0.8% of entry equity. A MES contract at index 7,000 has economic notional `7,000 × $5 = $35,000`. Merely fitting that notional under a 0.8% cap requires `35,000 / 0.008 = $4,375,000`, plus cost reserves. USD 5 million virtual capital is consequently a plausible *minimum-contract sizing* choice, not evidence of an edge. At higher prices or after losses, the contract-count gate can reject all orders. ES has ten times the MES notional and cannot use the same funding calculation unchanged.

Futures are variation-margin obligations, not ownership of a purchased currency balance. The existing cash-FX worst-case-loss proof therefore does **not automatically transfer**. A separate futures risk design must establish permitted prices/liabilities, reserves, margin/forced-liquidation behavior and contract-specific gap execution. Funding a notional and setting a stop does not prove that peak-to-trough drawdown from a profitable mark will remain below 1%, or that loss from entry cannot exceed its limit during an outage. No futures risk guarantee or activation is asserted by this feasibility note.

## Concrete requirements before a futures experiment

1. Acquire and pin multi-year actual trade/BBO or native M1/M5/M15 OHLC with original provider evidence, bar availability convention, timestamps and native expiry IDs. A purchased/vendor-authenticated source may be needed; none was purchased or authenticated here.
2. Define development/validation/untouched-final dates and a bounded, separate futures hypothesis family before reading strategy outcomes. This must not reuse an already inspected final sample for tuning.
3. Prefer per-contract bars. Freeze a causal liquidity/calendar roll rule and either flatten with actual fees before changing contracts or model both real leg prices and costs. Do not count a continuous-contract seam as profit. Back-adjusted prices may support selected features after careful availability review, but are not actual fills or notional values.
4. Implement and test integer contract sizing, multiplier/tick rounding, BBO or conservative fills, session/holiday and roll closures, contract-specific commission, mark-to-market accounting and the user risk limit. The current production cash-FX order gate remains unchanged.

The complete failed FX baseline research is separately recorded in `artifacts/intraday/mean_reversion/RESULTS.md`; this feasibility inspection does not replace it or establish a profitable futures strategy.
